Core Snapshot
Price, volatility, RSI, and short/medium-term moving averages
last_price Core
⚙️ How it's calculated
The closing price of the most recent trading day.
💡 Why it matters
The anchor for every other indicator. All percentages, distances, and comparisons are relative to this number.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME closed at ₹1,250 today. This is the last_price.
atr_pct Core
⚙️ How it's calculated
Simple average of the last 14 True Ranges divided by last_price, expressed as a percentage. True Range for each day = max(high − low, |high − prev_close|, |low − prev_close|).
💡 Why it matters
Measures daily volatility as a percentage of price. A 2% ATR means the stock typically moves ₹25 on a ₹1,250 stock. Low ATR stocks are calmer and suit smaller stop-losses; high ATR stocks need wider stops but offer larger swings.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's last 14 True Ranges average ₹31.25. ATR% = (31.25 / 1250) × 100 = 2.5%.
rsi_14 Core
⚙️ How it's calculated
14-period RSI using Wilder's smoothing. Seed: simple average of first 14 gains and losses. Then: avg_gain = (prev_avg_gain × 13 + current_gain) / 14. RSI = 100 − 100 / (1 + avg_gain / avg_loss).
💡 Why it matters
Identifies overbought (>70) and oversold (<30) conditions. RSI of 25 on a strong stock often signals a dip-buying opportunity; RSI above 80 warns of potential exhaustion.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME has been falling a week. avg_gain = 0.8, avg_loss = 2.1. RS = 0.38. RSI = 100 − 100/1.38 = 27.5 (oversold territory).
dma_20 Core
⚙️ How it's calculated
sum(close[-20:]) / 20 — simple moving average of the last 20 closing prices.
💡 Why it matters
Short-term trend gauge. Price above DMA-20 = short-term uptrend. A pullback to the 20-DMA in an uptrend is a classic mean-reversion buy setup.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's last 20 closes average ₹1,235. Since CMP (₹1,250) > DMA-20 (₹1,235), the short-term trend is up.
dma_50 Core
⚙️ How it's calculated
sum(close[-50:]) / 50 — simple moving average of the last 50 closing prices.
💡 Why it matters
Medium-term trend. The 50-DMA is widely watched by institutional traders. A stock above its 50-DMA is in a healthy medium-term trend. The 20/50 crossover generates golden/death cross signals.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's 50-day SMA = ₹1,190. CMP (₹1,250) is 5% above — solid medium-term uptrend.

Moving Averages
Long-term trend, EMA, and DMA slope momentum
ma_200 Moving Avg
⚙️ How it's calculated
Simple Moving Average of the last 200 closing prices. Requires ~10 months of data.
💡 Why it matters
The "big picture" trend divider. Stocks above 200-DMA are in long-term uptrends; below it, downtrends. Institutional funds often won't buy below the 200-DMA.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's 200-DMA = ₹1,100. CMP is ₹1,250 — 13.6% above. Long-term trend is firmly bullish.
ema_21 Moving Avg
⚙️ How it's calculated
21-period EMA seeded with the SMA of the first 21 closes, then: EMA = close × (2/22) + prev_EMA × (20/22), walked forward over all available data.
💡 Why it matters
More responsive than SMA — reacts faster to recent price changes. Swing traders use EMA-21 as a dynamic support/resistance line. In strong trends, pullbacks to EMA-21 are high-probability entries.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's EMA-21 = ₹1,242. CMP (₹1,250) is just ₹8 above — hugging its 21-EMA, a potential support bounce point.
slope_50dma_10d Moving Avg
⚙️ How it's calculated
% change of the 50-DMA over 10 trading days. Current 50-DMA = mean(close[-50:]). 10-day-ago 50-DMA = mean(close[-60:-10]). Slope = (current − old) / old × 100.
💡 Why it matters
A rising 50-DMA (positive slope) means the medium-term trend is accelerating. Flattening or negative slope warns of trend exhaustion. Distinguishes between a trending stock and one that's above its MA but losing momentum.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's current 50-DMA = ₹1,190, 10 days ago was ₹1,175. Slope = (1190−1175)/1175×100 = +1.28% (healthy upward drift).
slope_200dma_10d Moving Avg
⚙️ How it's calculated
Same method as slope_50dma_10d but for the 200-DMA. Current 200-DMA vs 200-DMA from 10 days ago.
💡 Why it matters
Captures momentum of the long-term trend. If the 200-DMA slope turns negative, the macro trend may be reversing — even if price is still above it.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's 200-DMA moved from ₹1,095 to ₹1,100 in 10 days. Slope = +0.46% (gently rising — long-term trend intact).

Price Action
Daily return, candle quality, and consolidation tightness
price_change_pct Price Action
⚙️ How it's calculated
(today's close − yesterday's close) / yesterday's close × 100
💡 Why it matters
Raw daily return. Feeds into price_direction and pattern derivations. Also useful for screening stocks with unusual single-day moves.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME closed ₹1,250 today, ₹1,230 yesterday. Change = (1250−1230)/1230×100 = +1.63%.
close_position Price Action
⚙️ How it's calculated
(close − day_low) / (day_high − day_low). Ranges 0.0 (closed at day low) to 1.0 (at day high). Returns 0.5 if high = low (circuit hit).
💡 Why it matters
Shows intraday buying/selling pressure. A close near 1.0 means buyers dominated; near 0.0 means sellers had the last word. Especially powerful combined with volume — high close_position + high volume = strong demand.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's day range ₹1,220–₹1,260, closed ₹1,250. Position = (1250−1220)/(1260−1220) = 0.75 (upper quartile — bullish).
base_tightness_20d Price Action
⚙️ How it's calculated
(highest close − lowest close) / lowest close × 100 over the 20 trading days before today (excludes today).
💡 Why it matters
Measures price consolidation. A tight base (<10%) followed by a breakout is a classic VCP (Volatility Contraction Pattern). Breakouts from tight bases tend to have stronger follow-through than wide-range breakouts.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's last 20 closes ranged ₹1,210–₹1,260. Tightness = (1260−1210)/1210×100 = 4.13% (very tight base — coiled for a move).

Price Levels: 10D
Short-term resistance and breakout proximity (≈10 calendar days)
high_10d 10D Levels
⚙️ How it's calculated
Highest intraday high over the last 7 trading days, excluding today (7 trading days ≈ 10 calendar days).
💡 Why it matters
Short-term resistance level. A close above high_10d on volume is a near-term breakout signal. Also used to set trailing stop-losses.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's highest high in the last 7 sessions = ₹1,265 (hit 3 days ago). Today's close (₹1,250) hasn't reclaimed it yet.
upside_to_10d_high 10D Levels
⚙️ How it's calculated
(high_10d − last_price) / last_price × 100
💡 Why it matters
Quantifies how far the stock needs to travel to reclaim its short-term high. Small upside (<2%) = near breakout; large upside (>5%) = significant pullback.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
(1265−1250)/1250×100 = 1.2% (ACME is just 1.2% from its 10-day high — near breakout).

Price Levels: 30D
Monthly range, resistance, support, and mean-reversion target
high_30d 30D Levels
⚙️ How it's calculated
Highest intraday high over the last 22 trading days, excluding today (22 trading days ≈ 30 calendar days).
💡 Why it matters
Monthly resistance level and primary mean-reversion target for swing trades. Stocks near their 30-day high are in a short-term uptrend; those far below are in a pullback. Used for GTT target setting.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's 30-day high = ₹1,290 (hit 12 days ago).
low_30d 30D Levels
⚙️ How it's calculated
Lowest intraday low over the last 22 trading days, excluding today.
💡 Why it matters
Monthly support level. A break below low_30d signals a new leg down; a bounce off it confirms support.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's 30-day low = ₹1,180 (hit 18 days ago during a market-wide dip).
range_30d_pct 30D Levels
⚙️ How it's calculated
(high_30d − low_30d) / low_30d × 100
💡 Why it matters
Measures the stock's monthly trading range. Wide (>15%) = high volatility, swing trade potential. Narrow (<5%) = consolidation, potential breakout setup.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
(1290−1180)/1180×100 = 9.32% (moderate range).
cmp_vs_30d_high_pct 30D Levels
⚙️ How it's calculated
(last_price − high_30d) / high_30d × 100
💡 Why it matters
Negative value shows how far below the monthly high the stock is trading. A reading of −8% means the stock has pulled back 8% from its recent high — useful for identifying dip-buy candidates.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
(1250−1290)/1290×100 = −3.10% (ACME is 3.1% below its 30-day high).
upside_to_30d_high 30D Levels
⚙️ How it's calculated
(high_30d − last_price) / last_price × 100
💡 Why it matters
Frames the same distance from the buyer's perspective — how much can I gain if the stock reclaims its recent high? Used for GTT target setting and risk-reward evaluation.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
(1290−1250)/1250×100 = 3.20% (3.2% upside to 30-day high).
cmp_vs_30d_low_pct 30D Levels
⚙️ How it's calculated
(last_price − low_30d) / low_30d × 100
💡 Why it matters
Shows how far above the monthly support the stock sits. Small value (<3%) = near 30-day floor, potential support bounce. Large value (>10%) = stock has rallied significantly off its recent low.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
(1250−1180)/1180×100 = 5.93% (about 6% above 30-day low — some cushion above support).

Price Levels: 52W
Annual range — the big psychological levels watched by all market participants
high_52w 52W Levels
⚙️ How it's calculated
Highest intraday high over the last 252 trading days (≈ 1 year).
💡 Why it matters
The primary psychological resistance. 52-week-high breakouts attract momentum traders and media attention. Stocks near their yearly high attract institutional accumulation.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's 52-week high = ₹1,350 (hit 4 months ago).
low_52w 52W Levels
⚙️ How it's calculated
Lowest intraday low over the last 252 trading days.
💡 Why it matters
Major support level. A stock near its 52-week low is either a deep value opportunity or a falling knife — other indicators help distinguish the two.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's 52-week low = ₹880 (hit 11 months ago during a market crash).
range_52w_pct 52W Levels
⚙️ How it's calculated
(high_52w − low_52w) / low_52w × 100
💡 Why it matters
Annual price range as a percentage. High range (>80%) = very volatile. Low range (<20%) = defensive/stable. Helps set position sizing — wider range stocks deserve smaller positions.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
(1350−880)/880×100 = 53.41% (ACME swung 53% in a year — moderately volatile).
cmp_vs_52w_high_pct 52W Levels
⚙️ How it's calculated
(last_price − high_52w) / high_52w × 100
💡 Why it matters
Shows how far below the yearly high the stock is. Within 5% = strong trend. 30%+ below may be structural decline or deep value territory.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
(1250−1350)/1350×100 = −7.41% (ACME is 7.4% below yearly high — moderate pullback).
cmp_vs_52w_low_pct 52W Levels
⚙️ How it's calculated
(last_price − low_52w) / low_52w × 100
💡 Why it matters
Shows the rally from the yearly floor. A stock 100%+ above its 52W low has been a strong performer. Useful for relative strength screening.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
(1250−880)/880×100 = 42.05% (ACME rallied 42% from its yearly low).
dip_from_52w_high_pct 52W Levels
⚙️ How it's calculated
(high_52w − last_price) / high_52w × 100
💡 Why it matters
Same magnitude as cmp_vs_52w_high_pct but expressed as a positive number. "This stock is in a 7.4% dip from its 52W high" is more intuitive than "−7.4%". Used directly in screening queries: WHERE dip_from_52w_high_pct BETWEEN 10 AND 25.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
(1350−1250)/1350×100 = 7.41%.

Volume
Baseline volume, relative activity, and low-volume pullback streak
avg_volume_20d Volume
⚙️ How it's calculated
Mean of daily volume over the last 20 trading days, excluding today.
💡 Why it matters
The baseline for judging whether today's volume is unusual. Also a liquidity filter — stocks with avg volume <100K shares/day are harder to enter/exit without slippage.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's average daily volume over 20 days = 1,850,000 shares.
rvol Volume
⚙️ How it's calculated
today's volume / avg_volume_20d — a relative volume ratio.
💡 Why it matters
Rvol >1.5 signals unusual institutional activity. Breakouts on rvol >2.0 have much higher follow-through rates. Rvol <0.5 on a down day is a positive sign — selling is on low conviction.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME traded 3,700,000 shares today. Rvol = 3,700,000 / 1,850,000 = 2.0 (double the average — significant activity).
consecutive_low_vol_down_days Volume
⚙️ How it's calculated
Starting from today, count consecutive trading days where: (a) close was lower than previous close, AND (b) that day's volume was <80% of its own 20-day average. Maximum look-back: 5 days.
💡 Why it matters
The healthy pullback detector. When a stock dips on declining volume, sellers are exhausted — supply is drying up. 3+ consecutive low-volume down days often precede a reversal. It's the opposite of high-volume selling (institutional distribution).
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME has been drifting lower for 3 days: each day closed red with volume at 65%, 72%, and 70% of the 20-day average. Result = 3 (supply drying up — watch for a bounce).

Derived Signals
Human-readable classifications built from the raw indicators above
ma_alignment Derived
⚙️ How it's calculated
Hierarchical check of price vs MAs:

FULL_BULL — CMP > DMA-20 > DMA-50 > MA-200
MEDIUM — CMP > DMA-50 > MA-200
LOW — CMP > MA-200 only
BEAR — CMP < MA-200

When fewer MAs are available (stock listed <200 days), uses whichever MAs exist.
💡 Why it matters
Captures the structural trend in a single label. FULL_BULL stocks are in confirmed multi-timeframe uptrends — highest win rate for trend-following strategies. BEAR stocks should only be bought for contrarian plays with strict risk management.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME: CMP ₹1,250 > DMA-20 ₹1,235 > DMA-50 ₹1,190 > MA-200 ₹1,100 → FULL_BULL.
price_direction Derived
⚙️ How it's calculated
UP — price_change_pct > +0.5%
DOWN — price_change_pct < −0.5%
FLAT — between −0.5% and +0.5%
💡 Why it matters
Simplifies today's price action into a label for pattern matching. Combined with volume_character to produce the pattern signal.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's price_change_pct = +1.63% → UP.
volume_character Derived
⚙️ How it's calculated
HIGH — rvol > 1.5
LOW — rvol < 0.8
NORMAL — between 0.8 and 1.5
💡 Why it matters
Labels today's volume relative to the norm. Combined with price_direction to classify the day's trading pattern.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's rvol = 2.0 → HIGH.
pattern Derived
⚙️ How it's calculated
Combination of price_direction + volume_character, evaluated in order:

CIRCUIT_HIT — high = low
CHURNING — rvol >2.0 but |price_change| <0.3%
STRONG_BUYING — UP + HIGH volume
WEAK_RALLY — UP + LOW volume
STRONG_SELLING — DOWN + HIGH volume
HEALTHY_PULLBACK — DOWN + LOW volume
NEUTRAL — everything else
💡 Why it matters
Condenses the day's price-volume action into one actionable label. HEALTHY_PULLBACK + FULL_BULL + near_support is the classic dip-buy setup. STRONG_SELLING + BEAR is a stay-away signal.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME: UP + HIGH → STRONG_BUYING (price rose on double-average volume — institutional demand).
is_near_support Derived
⚙️ How it's calculated
Returns True if CMP is within 2% of ANY of these levels:
• Prior consolidation high — max(close[-61:-21]) (the highest close 21–61 days ago)
• EMA-21
• DMA-50
💡 Why it matters
Previous resistance becomes support. When a stock pulls back to a known support level, the risk-reward for a long entry is favorable — tight stop below support, target at prior high.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME at ₹1,250, EMA-21 at ₹1,242. Distance = 0.64% <2% → True (near EMA-21 support).
is_breaking_out Derived
⚙️ How it's calculated
True when all three conditions are met:
1. close > max(close[-21:-1]) — new 20-day closing high
2. rvol > 1.5 — above-average volume
3. base_tightness_20d < 15% — came from a tight base
💡 Why it matters
A breakout from a tight base on high volume is one of the strongest bullish signals. Tight consolidation means supply has been absorbed; high volume means demand overwhelmed supply with conviction.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME closes ₹1,250, 20-day high close = ₹1,240, rvol = 2.0, tightness = 4.13%. All three conditions met → True (breakout confirmed).
days_since_last_breakout Derived
⚙️ How it's calculated
Looks back up to 60 trading days. For each past day, checks if it was a breakout candle (same 3 conditions as is_breaking_out). Returns trading days since the most recent one. Defaults to 60 if none found.
💡 Why it matters
Recent breakouts (<10 days) = early-stage momentum. Breakouts 20–40 days ago that haven't failed = sustained trend. No breakout in 60 days = range-bound or weak trend.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME had a breakout candle 8 days ago that hasn't reversed → 8 (early-stage momentum, still actionable for a pullback entry).

2-Year Historical Pattern
ATR history, dip frequency, bounce rates, and monthly trend consistency
avg_atr_pct_2y Historical
⚙️ How it's calculated
Mean of all rolling 14-day ATR% values computed over the full 2-year candle series using Wilder's smoothing.
💡 Why it matters
Gives the stock's 'normal' volatility personality. A stock with avg ATR% of 1.5% is fundamentally different to trade than one at 4%. Used for position sizing, stop-loss width, and comparing current ATR to historical norms.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's 2-year average ATR% = 2.3%. Today's ATR% is 2.5% — slightly above normal but not unusual.
atr_pct_min Historical
⚙️ How it's calculated
Minimum ATR% observed over the 2-year series.
💡 Why it matters
Extreme low volatility often precedes a big move (expansion after contraction). If current ATR% is near atr_pct_min, the stock is coiled and likely to break sharply.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's lowest ATR% in 2 years = 1.1% (during a low-volatility consolidation in mid-2024).
atr_pct_max Historical
⚙️ How it's calculated
Maximum ATR% observed over the 2-year series.
💡 Why it matters
Shows the stock's worst-case volatility. If current ATR% is approaching atr_pct_max, the stock is in an unusually volatile phase — widen stops or reduce position size.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's highest ATR% = 4.8% (during the March 2024 selloff). Current 2.5% is well below the extreme.
dip_events_total Historical
⚙️ How it's calculated
Over the 2-year series, count every instance where closing price dropped ≥5% from the rolling 30-day peak (highest close over the prior 30 bars).
💡 Why it matters
Tells you how often this stock experiences meaningful dips. 12 dip events in 2 years = one every ~6 weeks — frequent opportunities. 2–3 dips = very stable or untested.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME had 9 dip events in 2 years — roughly one every 11 weeks.
bounce_within_30d Historical
⚙️ How it's calculated
Of all dip events, how many recovered ≥4% from the trough within 30 trading days.
💡 Why it matters
Stocks that bounce quickly are ideal for dip-buying strategies with 4–6 week holding periods. High bounce_within_30d vs dip_events_total means strong buying support kicks in quickly.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
Of ACME's 9 dips, 6 recovered ≥4% within 30 days.
bounce_within_60d Historical
⚙️ How it's calculated
Of all dip events, how many recovered ≥4% within 60 trading days. Cumulative — includes all 30-day bounces plus any recovering between day 31 and day 60.
💡 Why it matters
Captures slower recoveries. Some quality stocks take 6–8 weeks. If bounce_within_60d is much higher than bounce_within_30d, the stock recovers — just slowly.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
Of ACME's 9 dips, 8 recovered within 60 days (6 fast + 2 slow). Only 1 led to sustained decline.
bounce_rate_30d Historical
⚙️ How it's calculated
bounce_within_30d / dip_events_total. Ranges 0 to 1.
💡 Why it matters
Probability that a dip recovers quickly. Rate >0.7 = 70%+ of historical dips bounced within a month — strong evidence for a dip-buying edge. Below 0.4 = dips tend to persist.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
6 / 9 = 0.667 (67% of ACME's dips recovered within 30 days).
bounce_rate_60d Historical
⚙️ How it's calculated
bounce_within_60d / dip_events_total. Ranges 0 to 1.
💡 Why it matters
Overall recovery probability. 0.9+ = stock almost always recovers — only a question of time. Combining with bounce_rate_30d gives the speed profile.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
8 / 9 = 0.889 (89% of dips recovered within 60 days — high confidence that dip-buying works).
avg_recovery_days Historical
⚙️ How it's calculated
For all dip events that recovered ≥4% from trough, the average number of trading days from trough to recovery.
💡 Why it matters
Sets holding period expectations. Avg recovery of 15 days → a 30-day position covers it comfortably. 45 days → more patience and capital commitment needed.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's 8 recovered dips averaged 18.5 trading days to recover (≈3.5 calendar weeks).
max_recovery_days Historical
⚙️ How it's calculated
The longest recovery in the 2-year series, in trading days.
💡 Why it matters
The worst-case scenario for a dip buyer. How long you might have to wait in the worst case. Useful for setting maximum holding period rules.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's slowest recovery took 52 trading days (about 2.5 months — during a prolonged market correction).
avg_dip_depth_pct Historical
⚙️ How it's calculated
Average percentage drop from the 30-day rolling peak to the trough, across all dip events.
💡 Why it matters
If avg depth is 8% and the stock has already dipped 7%, most of the downside may be done. If avg is 8% and it's only dipped 5%, there may be more to come.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's average dip depth = 7.8% from the 30-day peak.
max_dip_depth_pct Historical
⚙️ How it's calculated
The deepest single dip (peak to trough) in the 2-year series.
💡 Why it matters
Worst-case drawdown from a dip event. Stress-tests stop-loss levels — if max dip was 18% and your stop is at 10%, you would have been stopped out during the worst correction.
📝 Example (ACME @ ₹1,250)
ACME's deepest dip = 16.2% (during a sector-wide selloff — outlier event).
positive_months Historical
⚙️ How it's calculated
Over ~24 months of data, count months where the last close > first open (month ended higher than it started).

Deep Dive: Dip Detection Logic
Step-by-step walkthrough of how bounce_within_30d and bounce_within_60d are computed

How _detect_bounces() works

The dip detection is a sliding scanner across the 2-year close price series. Here is exactly what happens at each step.

1
Find the rolling peak

At each trading day i, compute the highest close over the prior 30 trading days (not including day i):

rolling_peak = max(closes[i - 30 : i])

This is the "recent high" the stock has been trading at.

2
Check if today is a dip (≥5% threshold)

Compare day i's close against that rolling peak: 

dip_pct = ((rolling_peak - closes[i]) / rolling_peak) * 100

If dip_pct ≥ 5%, it counts as a dip event. Trigger: "the stock closed ≥5% below its highest close of the past 30 trading days."

3
Find the actual trough

Once a dip is detected at day i, search the next 30 days for the lowest close — the real bottom:

trough = closes[i]
for j in range(i, i + 30):
    if closes[j] < trough:
        trough = closes[j]

The day the 5% threshold was first breached may not be the bottom — the stock could keep falling a few more days.

4
Check for 30-day recovery

Starting from the day after the trough, check if the stock recovered ≥4% from that trough within 30 days. If yes → bounce_within_30d += 1.

5
Check for 60-day recovery (if no 30-day recovery)

If not recovered in 30 days, scan days 31–60 from the trough. If found → bounce_within_60d += 1 (counts slow-but-valid recoveries).

6
Skip ahead to avoid double-counting

After processing a dip event, the scanner jumps forward past the recovery point (or past 60 days if no recovery) so the same dip isn't counted twice.

Concrete example with numbers

# Day 1–30: ACME closes hover around ₹1,300
#           rolling peak at day 30 = ₹1,310

# Day 31: ACME closes at ₹1,240
dip_pct = (1310 - 1240) / 1310 * 100  # = 5.34%  ← ≥5%, DIP DETECTED

# Days 31–60: Search for trough
# Day 35: closes at ₹1,205 (lowest)  ← this is the trough

# Days 36–65: Search for 30-day recovery from ₹1,205
# Day 48: closes at ₹1,260
recovery = (1260 - 1205) / 1205 * 100  # = 4.56%  ← ≥4%, BOUNCED

# Result:
dip_events      += 1
bounce_within_30d += 1
recovery_days    = 48 - 35   # = 13 days
dip_depth        = (1310 - 1205) / 1310 * 100  # = 8.02%

# Scanner jumps to day 49, looks for next dip

The dip is tracked as "close dropped ≥5% below the best close of the prior 30 trading days" — it is a close-to-close measurement, not intraday.
