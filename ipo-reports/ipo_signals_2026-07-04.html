🎯 Where to invest

This report scores each fresh listing on the only question that builds wealth from IPOs: "Is this a multi-year compounding machine, run by aligned owners, that I can still buy with the upside ahead of me?" A profitable company that cannot compound — or one where the easy re-rating has already happened — is not an opportunity.

The two lenses

1 · Quality score (runbook, /10) — the safety check. Is it a real, non-fraudulent business, not a value trap? Five lenses (business, growth, profitability, IPO structure, valuation), added up.

2 · Opportunity score (QGLP-A, /10) — the headline. Will it compound for me, and is the upside still on the table? This is a gated score, not an additive one — that's the whole point.

QGLP-A — the 4 pillars

A · ENGINE (0–4) · GATING Does it actually compound?

A1 growth + runway (>20% growth into a large under-penetrated TAM) and A2 reinvestment ability (can it redeploy profit at high ROIC, or is the cash paid out?). If A ≤ 1, the whole score is capped at 4 — a profitable non-compounder is a dividend stock, not an opportunity.

B · MOAT & LONGEVITY (0–2) Will the high return persist?

Durable moat (intangibles, switching costs, cost edge) and an end-market that isn't structurally decaying. No moat → returns mean-revert → no longevity.

C · ALIGNMENT (0–2) Will the compounding accrue to ME?

Founder-owner with fresh capital going into the business = 2. Pure OFS cash-out / PE exit / government divestment = 0. In an OFS, the seller knows more than you.

D · PRICE & ASYMMETRY (0–2) Is the upside still on the table?

Margin of safety vs realistic downside. If the stock already sits at/near fair value or street targets after a big run, the asymmetry is gone even if the business is fine.

Structural vetoes (cap, never add): Government/PSU control → cap 5 · Pure-OFS no fresh capital → cap 6 · Hot-window froth listing → cap 6 · No engine (A ≤ 1) → cap 4.
Verdict bands: 8–10 CONVICTION (size up) · 6–7.5 OPPORTUNITY-conditional (half-size / wait for the condition) · 4–5.5 NOT AN OPPORTUNITY · <4 AVOID.

The reading list — how great investors think

100 Baggers — Christopher Mayer
"Twin engines": high sustained EPS growth × multiple expansion, on a long reinvestment runway, run by owner-operators. You don't get 100x from a dividend payer.
The Three-Legged Stool — Chuck Akre
Compounding business × honest owner-managers × a runway to reinvest free cash above cost of capital. Leg 3 is the one everyone forgets.
QGLP — Raamdeo Agrawal
Quality × Growth × Longevity × Price. "Price comes last." Longevity turns growth into wealth.
One Up on Wall Street — Peter Lynch
Categorise first. The big money is in fast growers with a long runway — not stalwarts or slow growers wearing a quality mask.
Coffee Can Investing — Saurabh Mukherjea
Avoid PSUs: govt-vs-shareholder conflict, sub-cost-of-capital capex, no value-creation engine. Cheapness doesn't compensate.
IPO long-run studies — Jay Ritter
IPOs underperform peers over 3 years, worst in hot "windows." The seller knows more than you — especially in an OFS. Default skepticism is rational.
The Little Book That Builds Wealth — Pat Dorsey
Moats (intangibles, switching costs, network, cost edge) are why high returns persist. No moat → mean reversion.

What it does

Sai Parenterals makes pharmaceutical formulations — injectables (its heritage), tablets, capsules, syrups and ointments — for India and export markets, and runs a fast-growing CDMO (contract manufacturing) arm. Post-IPO it owns Noumed Pharmaceuticals (Australia), an OTC-medicines business, via a Singapore holdco — its lever into regulated Western OTC distribution.

Future prospects · why the IPO · customers

Why they listed: ₹409 cr issue = ~₹285 cr fresh (70%) + ~₹124 cr OFS. The fresh money repays bridge/term loans taken to acquire Noumed — i.e. capital went into the business, and the OFS was entirely non-promoter early investors, not the founders. That's the alignment you want.

The engine: CDMO revenue scaled ₹5.3 cr → ₹31 cr (FY23→FY25), ~6× in two years. The transformational catalyst landed 1 Jul 2026: Noumed signed an exclusive ₹1,300 cr (AUD 202M) OTC supply deal with an Australian pharmacy network — 7.5 years, ~₹173 cr/yr run-rate, roughly a full current quarter of revenue from a single contract, effective immediately. The catch: FY25 base is tiny — revenue ₹164 cr, PAT ₹14.5 cr, EBITDA ₹39 cr. Much of the story is forward, and execution on the Noumed deal is unproven in the P&L yet.

Street view & recent news

No formal post-listing institutional coverage yet. IPO-stage valuation was already steep — ~88–111× P/E at the issue price; peers Gland Pharma and Innova Captab trade far lower. The stock is now +58% above issue and at an all-time high, so the multiple is richer still. News flow is strongly positive: the ₹1,300 cr Noumed OTC deal (1 Jul) and an analyst/investor meet (2 Jul) are what drove the ATH breakout this signal fired on.

Promoters & management GOOD

Founder-promoter group retained control — the OFS came from non-promoter early investors booking profit, not the founders. Fresh issue funds the Noumed acquisition rather than a cash-out. This is a founder-run growth company deploying fresh capital into an offshore expansion — aligned, though the offshore/Singapore-holdco structure adds complexity to watch.

What it does

Founded in Rajkot in 2006, Omnitech makes high-precision engineered components and assemblies for three end-markets: energy / oilfield (~50%), motion control & automation (~30%) and industrial equipment (~20%). It is an export-led precision-machining house — ~75% of revenue is international (USA, Canada, Germany) — supplying marquee names including Halliburton, Weatherford and Suzlon.

Future prospects · why the IPO · customers

Why they listed: ₹583 cr issue = ₹418 cr fresh (72%) + ₹165 cr OFS by promoter Udaykumar Parekh; promoter stake steps down 94% → 74%. Fresh capital funds capacity expansion — money into the business, promoter still deeply invested.

The engine: the standout of the batch. Order book jumped to ₹1,765 cr (Sep-25) from ₹284 cr (Mar-25) — a ~4× book-to-bill — driven by a single ₹1,040 cr energy order. FY26 revenue +49% to ₹511 cr, PAT ₹79 cr; Q3 FY26 net profit +171%. Real revenue visibility for years, into the global oilfield-services capex up-cycle.

The catch: that same ₹1,040 cr order is a customer-concentration risk — a majority of the order book rides one client. Precision-machining is also cyclical and tied to energy capex.

Street view & recent news

Ironically it listed at an ~11% discount (₹202 vs issue) on weak demand — then compounded +178% as the order book and profit surge became visible. Trailing P/E has climbed from ~62× (Mar) to ~89× (early Jul) — the market has fully re-rated the growth. Formal broker targets are thin; five domestic mutual funds took the anchor book, which is a quality tell. Q3 FY26's +171% profit print is the recent driver.

Promoters & management GOOD

Founder-promoter Udaykumar Parekh retains ~74% post-issue and raised mostly fresh capital for capacity — strongly aligned, real skin in the game. The OFS slice is a partial, measured trim, not a cash-out. Watch customer concentration and related-party/export-pricing disclosures as the order book converts.
